Kansas City jazz is a hard-swinging, bluesy jazz style that developed in Kansas City, Kansas and Kansas City, Missouri and the surrounding metropolitan area during the 1930s, marking the transition from structured big band style to the improvisation of Bebop. The style is bracketed by Count Basie, who signed with Bennie Moten's Kansas City Orchestra in 1929, and Kansas City native Charlie Parker, who ushered in Bebop in the 1940s. Kansas City is regarded as one of the most popular 'cradles of jazz', alongside New Orleans, Chicago, St. Louis, Pittsburgh, Philadelphia, and New York City.
