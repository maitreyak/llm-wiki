The passage is an idiosyncratic account of loan modification in the United States, defining it as the systematic alteration of mortgage loan agreements (reducing interest rates, monthly payments or principal balances) to prevent foreclosure, and dating its U.S. practice to the 2008 housing market crash involving Washington Mutual, Chase/JP Morgan Chase and MERS. It claims modifications arose after Midwestern note holders petitioned the U.S. Department of Housing, the IRS and President Obama, that HAMP/HARP were introduced in 2009, and that 'watch dog' bodies such as the FBI Mortgage Fraud Department, IRS, Comptroller, FTC/Consumer Protection Bureau came into existence after the crash. It further asserts that the true cause of the crash was staff across the real estate chain wrongfully claiming homeowners' escrow and equity, aided by balloon mortgages designed to make note holders default, and notes historical Depression-era loan transfers, assumptions and bailouts as predecessors, plus the illegality of charging for a modification.
