The history of the Kansas City metropolitan area begins in the 19th century, when Frenchmen from St. Louis, Missouri traveled up the Missouri River to trap furs and trade with Native Americans. Straddling the Missouri–Kansas border at the confluence of the Kansas and Missouri Rivers, the area was strategic for commerce and security; Kansas City, Missouri was founded in 1838 and overtook its rival Westport to become the predominant city west of St. Louis. The region played a major role in westward expansion, with the Santa Fe and Oregon trails running through it, and after Kansas was opened to Euro-American settlement in 1854 the Missouri–Kansas border became the first battlefield of the conflict in the American Civil War.
