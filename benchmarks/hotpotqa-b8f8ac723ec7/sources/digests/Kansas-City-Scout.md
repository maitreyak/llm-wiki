Kansas City Scout is one of the largest electronic traffic management systems in the United States, created in 2000 as a bi-state initiative between the Missouri and Kansas Departments of Transportation for the Kansas City Metropolitan Area at a cost of $43 million, 80-90% funded by the Federal Highway Administration. It uses electronic message boards and cameras on major highways to display traffic information and detect problems, with priority segments chosen by accident-rate studies since covering all metro highways would be too costly. Future plans include extending coverage to I-635 and expanding on I-435, especially in the northern metro. The system is named after the Kansas City Scout Statue in Penn Valley Park overlooking Downtown Kansas City.
