# 2008 Housing Market Crash

> The 2008 collapse of the U.S. housing market, described in the passage as the starting point of loan modification practice and as driven by lenders and real estate staff seeking homeowners' escrow and equity.

## Key Facts

- The passage dates the practice of [[concepts/Loan-Modification-in-the-United-States]] in the United States to the 2008 crash of the housing market. [src:Loan-modification-in-the-United-States]
- Institutions named in connection with the crash include [[organizations/Washington-Mutual]], [[organizations/Chase-Home-Finance]], [[organizations/JP-Morgan-Chase]] and other contributors such as MERS. [src:Loan-modification-in-the-United-States]
- The passage asserts that the real prize and reason for the housing crash was profit obtained from mortgage holders' escrow. [src:Loan-modification-in-the-United-States]
- It claims lending institutions provided mortgages to people they knew would eventually lose them, with loan holders purchasing [[concepts/Balloon-Mortgage]] products designed to make fast money off the note holder. [src:Loan-modification-in-the-United-States]
- The passage says the key to the crash was not the house but the loan product used and the interest and money accrued from note holders. [src:Loan-modification-in-the-United-States]
