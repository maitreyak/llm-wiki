# Kansas City Scout

> One of the United States' largest electronic traffic management systems, created in 2000 as a bi-state Missouri–Kansas initiative for the Kansas City Metropolitan Area.

## Key Facts

- Kansas City Scout is one of the United States' largest electronic traffic management systems. [src:Kansas-City-Scout]
- Kansas City Scout was created in 2000 as part of a bi-state initiative between the [[organizations/Missouri-Department-of-Transportation]] and the [[organizations/Kansas-Department-of-Transportation]]. [src:Kansas-City-Scout]
- The Kansas City Scout project cost $43 million. [src:Kansas-City-Scout]
- The system employs electronic boards placed on major highways throughout the metropolitan area that display traffic information when needed, such as for an accident, lane closure, or highway closure. [src:Kansas-City-Scout]
- The system has cameras on those billboards to automatically detect traffic problems. [src:Kansas-City-Scout]
- Because covering all major highways in the Kansas City metro would be too expensive, studies were conducted to determine which highways or segments have priority due to higher accident rates. [src:Kansas-City-Scout]
- Future plans are to extend coverage to I-635, cover I-435 more extensively (especially in the northern areas of the metro), and cover other major city highways. [src:Kansas-City-Scout]
- Kansas City Scout was named after the [[places/Kansas-City-Scout-Statue]] in [[places/Penn-Valley-Park]], which overlooks [[places/Downtown-Kansas-City]]. [src:Kansas-City-Scout]
