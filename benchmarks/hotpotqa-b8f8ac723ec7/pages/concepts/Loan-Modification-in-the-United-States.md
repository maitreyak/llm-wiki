# Loan Modification in the United States

> Loan modification is the systematic alteration of mortgage loan agreements — reducing interest rates, monthly payments or principal balances — used in the United States to relieve borrowers and prevent foreclosure.

## Key Facts

- Loan modification is the systematic alteration of mortgage loan agreements that helps those having problems making payments by reducing interest rates, monthly payments or principal balances. [src:Loan-modification-in-the-United-States]
- Lending institutions could make one or more of these changes to relieve financial pressure on borrowers and prevent the condition of foreclosure. [src:Loan-modification-in-the-United-States]
- Loan modifications have been practiced in the United States since the 2008 crash of the housing market, associated with [[organizations/Washington-Mutual]], [[organizations/Chase-Home-Finance]], [[organizations/JP-Morgan-Chase]] and other contributors such as MERS. [src:Loan-modification-in-the-United-States]
- The passage states that note holders from the Midwest reached out through the democratic process to the [[organizations/United-States-Department-of-Housing]], the [[organizations/Internal-Revenue-Service]] and [[people/Barack-Obama]] asking for help to keep their homes, leading to the birth of modifications. [src:Loan-modification-in-the-United-States]
- Before modifications existed, loan holders experiencing crisis used loan assumptions and loan transfers to keep the note, as in the 1930s. [src:Loan-modification-in-the-United-States]
- In 2009, [[concepts/Home-Affordable-Refinance-Program]], [[concepts/Home-Affordable-Modification-Program]] and modifications were introduced to stop the victimization of note holders. [src:Loan-modification-in-the-United-States]
- The passage states it had been unlawful for an attorney to charge for a modification, and unlawful for banking staff to modify terms to increase a mortgage or switch the lending product to a balloon in an effort to make a homeowner foreclose. [src:Loan-modification-in-the-United-States]
