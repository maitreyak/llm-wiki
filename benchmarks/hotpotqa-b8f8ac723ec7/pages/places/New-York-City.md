# New York City

> American city from which the first season of Hamish & Andy's Gap Year was broadcast weekly, the base of index Magazine, the setting of the 1994 film It Could Happen to You, one of the cradles of jazz, home to Brooklyn where Kingston Morning was partly recorded, the founding city of the New York Society of Model Engineers, and the location of the Random House Tower.

## Key Facts

- The first season of [[media/Hamish-Andys-Gap-Year]] was broadcast weekly from New York City during the hosts' ten-episode visit to America [src:Hamish-and-Andys-Gap-Year]
- [[people/David-LaChapelle]]'s exhibit [[events/Artists-and-Prostitutes-1985-2005]], featuring [[people/Amanda-Lepore]], was held in New York City. [src:Amanda-Lepore]
- [[people/Armand-Castelmary]] died in New York City on 10 February 1897. [src:Armand-Castelmary]
- The [[organizations/Metropolitan-Opera-House]], where Castelmary died onstage during a performance of [[works/Martha]], is in New York City. [src:Armand-Castelmary]
- [[people/Criss-Angel]] began his career in New York City before moving his base of operations to the [[places/Las-Vegas-Valley]]. [src:Criss-Angel]
- Jericho Cane, the protagonist of [[works/End-of-Days]], is a former New York Police Department detective. [src:End-of-Days-film]
- Christie Opara, the main character of [[people/Dorothy-Uhnak]]'s novel [[works/The-Ledger]], was a white New York Police detective. [src:Get-Christie-Love]
- In New York City, the [[organizations/Great-Eastern-Conventions]] shows filled the gap between the mid-1980s demise of the annual [[events/Comic-Art-Convention]] and [[events/Creation-Conventions]], and the establishment of the [[events/Big-Apple-Comic-Con]] in 1996. [src:Great-Eastern-Conventions]
- From 1993 to 1995, Great Eastern hosted two New York City shows annually at the [[places/Jacob-K-Javits-Convention-Center]]. [src:Great-Eastern-Conventions]
- The Indian romantic comedy [[works/I-Love-NY]] was extensively shot in New York City, along with [[places/Mumbai]] and [[places/Bangkok]]. [src:I-Love-NY-2015-film]
- [[media/index-Magazine]] was a prominent New York City based publication running from 1996 to late 2005. [src:Index-Magazine]
- index Magazine featured interviews with not-so-famous New York personalities such as Queen Itchie or Ducky Doolittle. [src:Index-Magazine]
- The 1994 film [[media/It-Could-Happen-to-You]] tells the story of a New York City police officer who wins the lottery. [src:It-Could-Happen-to-You-1994-film]
- [[works/Just-Another-Romantic-Wrestling-Comedy]] premiered in [[places/New-Jersey]] and New York City on December 1, 2006. [src:Just-Another-Romantic-Wrestling-Comedy]
- New York City is listed among the popular "cradles of jazz" along with Kansas City, [[places/New-Orleans]], [[places/Chicago]], [[places/St-Louis-Missouri]], [[places/Pittsburgh]], and [[places/Philadelphia]]. [src:Kansas-City-jazz]
- [[works/Kingston-Morning]], [[people/Dave-Eggar]]'s 4th solo release, was partly recorded in Brooklyn, New York. [src:Kingston-Morning]
- DJ [[people/Mick-Batyske]] has performed in venues in New York City, among other international cities. [src:Mick-DJ]
- The Society incorporated in order to obtain a permit to use a lake in New York City's [[places/Central-Park]] for model motor boat races. [src:New-York-Society-of-Model-Engineers]
- The 2003 romantic comedy [[works/Nola]] depicts a young woman trying to survive in New York City while looking for her birth father. [src:Nola-film]
- Nola premiered in New York City on July 23, 2004. [src:Nola-film]
- The [[places/Random-House-Tower]], a 52-story mixed-use tower also known as the Park Imperial Apartments, is located in New York City. [src:Random-House-Tower]
- The Random House Tower's publisher entrance is on Broadway in New York City, while its apartment complex entrance is on West 56th Street. [src:Random-House-Tower]
- The rock band [[organizations/Suffrajett]] is from New York City. [src:Suffrajett]
