# Djamaâ el Kebir

> The Great Mosque of Algiers, a mosque in Algiers, Algeria built in 1097 and one of the few remaining examples of Almoravid architecture.

## Key Facts

- The Great Mosque of Algiers (Arabic: الجامع الكبير, "Jemaa Kebir") or "Djama'a al-Kebir", meaning Great Mosque, is a mosque in [[places/Algiers]], Algeria. [src:Djamaa-el-Kebir]
- The mosque is located very close to Algiers Harbor. [src:Djamaa-el-Kebir]
- An inscription on the mosque's minbar (منبر) or pulpit testifies to the fact that the mosque was built in 1097. [src:Djamaa-el-Kebir]
- It is one of the few remaining examples of [[concepts/Almoravid-architecture]]. [src:Djamaa-el-Kebir]
- It is the oldest mosque in [[places/Algiers]] and is said to be the oldest mosque in Algeria after [[places/Sidi-Okba-Mosque]]. [src:Djamaa-el-Kebir]
- The mosque was built under sultan [[people/Ali-ibn-Yusuf]]. [src:Djamaa-el-Kebir]
- Its minaret dates from 1332 (1324 in some sources) and was built by the Ziyyanid Sultan of Tlemcen. [src:Djamaa-el-Kebir]
- The gallery at the outside of the mosque was built in 1840, as a consequence of a complete reconstruction of the street by the French. [src:Djamaa-el-Kebir]
